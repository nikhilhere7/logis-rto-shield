# M3 — Audit & Operational Intelligence

M3 checks every courier invoice line against the merchant's own rate card and parcel data, reconciles COD
cash the courier collected against what it paid back, and turns every disputable rupee into an
`AuditFinding` the merchant can review in the API or send to the courier as a CSV.

It builds on M2: the audit reads the `ShipmentLedger` (one row per AWB with real weight, destination zone,
outcome and COD figures) rather than re-deriving those facts.

## 2. The `AuditFinding` model

`AuditFinding` extends `core.TenantModel`, so it inherits the `merchant` foreign key (reverse name
`merchant.auditfindings`) and the `for_merchant()` lookup that every query uses. Findings are never edited
by hand: `run_audit()` deletes a merchant's findings and writes them again.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `kind` | One of the nine categories below |
| `courier`, `courier_name` | Courier that billed the line, or that owes the COD |
| `awb_number` | Parcel |
| `invoice_id`, `line_ref` | Invoice line the finding came from; blank for COD findings |
| `billed_inr` | Invoice findings: what the courier billed. COD findings: what the courier remitted |
| `expected_inr` | Invoice findings: what the rate card allows. COD findings: the COD amount due |
| `claim_inr` | Amount to dispute |
| `detail` | One-line explanation, e.g. `Billed 2 x 500g slabs, parcel is 1` |
| `created_at` | When the audit run created it |

Findings are listed by `claim_inr`, largest first, with `id` as the tie-break so pages are stable.

### Finding categories

| `kind` | Label | Claim |
|---|---|---|
| `unknown_awb` | Billed for a parcel we never shipped | Whole line |
| `duplicate_line` | Same AWB billed more than once | Whole repeated line |
| `weight_inflated` | Billed weight higher than the parcel | Billed − expected |
| `wrong_zone` | Billed for a costlier zone | Billed − expected |
| `rto_charge_not_due` | RTO charge on a parcel that was not returned | Billed − expected |
| `cod_fee_not_due` | COD fee that should not apply | Billed − expected |
| `other_overcharge` | Total higher than the rate card allows | Billed − expected |
| `cod_not_remitted` | COD collected but never paid to us | Full COD amount |
| `cod_short_paid` | COD paid, but less than collected | COD due − remitted |

The first seven are **billing** findings; the last two are **COD** findings.

## 3. Invoice pricing

Constants in `audit/engine.py`: `GST_RATE = 0.18`, `TOLERANCE_INR = 1.0`, `COD_GRACE_DAYS = 21`.
All money is calculated in `Decimal` and rounded half-up to the paisa.

For each of the merchant's invoice lines, sorted by invoice date and then line reference:

1. **Unknown AWB.** If the AWB is not in the merchant's ledger, the whole line is claimed as `unknown_awb`.
2. **Duplicate.** The first line for an AWB is the original bill; every later line for that AWB is claimed
   in full as `duplicate_line`. Repeated lines are not priced.
3. **Rate card.** The rate is looked up by *(courier on the invoice line, the parcel's destination zone
   from the ledger)*. If the parcel has no zone or the courier has no rate for that zone, the line is
   counted in `lines_not_verifiable` and nothing is claimed.
4. **Expected charges**, using the parcel's real chargeable weight (`weight_slabs` from the ledger, where
   1 slab = 500 g of the larger of dead and volumetric weight):

   ```
   freight  = first_500g_inr + (slabs − 1) × addl_500g_inr
   cod_fee  = max(cod_min_inr, order_value × cod_fee_pct / 100)       only for delivered COD parcels
   rto_fee  = freight × rto_charge_pct_of_freight / 100               only for returned parcels
   expected = freight + cod_fee + rto_fee
   ```

5. **GST.** Courier invoices carry GST as its own column (`gst_inr`), and their total is
   freight + COD + RTO + GST. The expected amount is multiplied by 1.18 only when the line's `gst_inr` is
   greater than zero, so a line billed without GST is compared without GST.
6. **Compare.** `claim = total_billed_inr − expected`.
   - More than ₹1 under expected: counted in `lines_underbilled`, no finding.
   - Within ₹1: correct, no finding.
   - More than ₹1 over: one finding for the whole overcharge, with the reason chosen in this order:
     billed slabs above real slabs → `weight_inflated`; billed zone different from the destination zone →
     `wrong_zone`; RTO freight billed on a parcel that was not returned → `rto_charge_not_due`; COD fee more
     than ₹1 above the fee due → `cod_fee_not_due`; otherwise → `other_overcharge`.

A wrong-zone line is priced at the **destination** zone's rate; the billed zone only decides the reason.

**Worked example (from the tests).** A delivered COD parcel of ₹1,299 in zone A, rate card A =
₹30 first 500 g, 2% COD fee with a ₹30 minimum: expected = (30 + max(30, 25.98)) × 1.18 = **₹70.80**.
Billed at zone B rates (₹40 freight) = (40 + 30) × 1.18 = ₹82.60, so the `wrong_zone` claim is **₹11.80**.

## 4. COD reconciliation and the 21-day grace period

Only **delivered COD parcels** are checked. The ledger already holds, per parcel, the COD due (the order
value) and the total remitted against that AWB.

- The **latest statement date** is the most recent `remittance_date` across all of the merchant's
  remittances. The **cutoff** is that date minus the grace period (21 days by default).
- **Nothing remitted:** if the parcel was delivered (IST date) on or before the cutoff, it is
  `cod_not_remitted` and the full COD amount is claimed. If it was delivered after the cutoff, or has no
  delivery time, it counts in `cod_pending_not_yet_due` and is not claimed.
- **Partly remitted:** if COD due − remitted is more than ₹1, it is `cod_short_paid` for the gap. The
  grace period does not apply to short payments.
- **No remittance statement at all:** nothing is claimed; every unpaid parcel counts as pending.

Example: delivered 4 March, latest statement 25 March (21 days later) → claimed. Latest statement 24 March
(20 days later) → still pending.

## 5. API

All endpoints use the existing `MerchantAPIView` from `ingestion/views.py`: the user must be logged in
(session or basic authentication), and the merchant always comes from the logged-in user, never from the
request. A user with no merchant gets 403.

| Method | Path | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| POST | `/api/audit/run/` | Rebuilds the merchant's findings and returns the run statistics. Optional `?cod_grace_days=` (whole number ≥ 0, default 21); anything else → 400 |
| GET | `/api/audit/summary/` | Totals and breakdowns (below) |
| GET | `/api/audit/findings/` | Plain JSON list, largest claim first. Optional `?kind=` (must be one of the nine kinds, else 400), `?courier=` (exact courier name), `?limit=` (default 50, max 500) and `?offset=` (default 0); invalid or negative values → 400 |
| GET | `/api/audit/disputes.csv` | Dispute file download |

**Run statistics** (`POST /api/audit/run/`): `lines_checked`, `lines_not_verifiable`, `lines_underbilled`,
`cod_delivered_parcels`, `cod_pending_not_yet_due`, `findings`, `by_kind` (count per kind) and
`claim_total_inr`.

**Summary** (`GET /api/audit/summary/`):

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `total_courier_billed_inr` | Sum of all the merchant's invoice line totals |
| `billing_overcharge_inr`, `billing_findings` | Total and count of the seven billing kinds |
| `overcharge_pct_of_billed` | Billing overcharge as a % of total billed (`null` if nothing billed) |
| `cod_missing_inr`, `cod_findings` | Total and count of `cod_not_remitted` + `cod_short_paid` |
| `total_recoverable_inr` | Billing overcharge + COD missing |
| `findings` | Total number of findings |
| `by_kind` | `[{kind, label, findings, claim_inr}]` |
| `by_courier` | `[{courier, findings, claim_inr}]` |
| `by_courier_kind` | `[{courier, kind, label, findings, claim_inr}]` |

**Finding rows** (`GET /api/audit/findings/`): `id`, `kind`, `label`, `courier_name`, `awb_number`,
`invoice_id`, `line_ref`, `billed_inr`, `expected_inr`, `claim_inr`, `detail`, `created_at` (ISO 8601).

## 6. Dispute CSV

`GET /api/audit/disputes.csv` downloads `logis_dispute_claims.csv` (`Content-Type: text/csv;
charset=utf-8`, `Content-Disposition: attachment`). It contains every finding for the merchant, largest
claim first, with these columns:

`Kind, Reason, Courier, AWB, Invoice ID, Line ref, Billed (INR), Expected (INR), Claim (INR), Detail`

- The file starts with a UTF-8 byte-order mark so Excel opens it correctly.
- Amounts are written from the stored decimals, always with two decimal places (`1299.00`).
- Rows are written with Python's `csv` module, so commas and quotes in text are escaped correctly.

## 7. Ingestion commit integration

`CommitView.post` (`POST /api/ingestion/uploads/<id>/commit/`) now runs these steps in **one**
`transaction.atomic()` block:

1. Load the validated rows.
2. Save the upload batch as `LOADED` with its quality report.
3. `rebuild_ledger(merchant)`.
4. `run_audit(merchant)` with the default 21-day grace period.

If any step fails, the whole block rolls back: the loaded rows, the batch status, the ledger and the
findings all stay as they were. The response is the batch summary plus `"ledger"` and `"audit"` (the run
statistics).

Because every commit re-runs the audit over all of the merchant's data, the findings are always in step
with the latest uploads. For example, an invoice for an AWB whose shipment arrives in a later upload stops
being `unknown_awb` as soon as the shipments file is committed.

The audit was run on the merchant whose data came in through the ingestion pipeline (merchant 2,
"Upload Test").

| COD missing | ₹67,253.00 (89 findings) |

| Dispute CSV | 1,773 data rows, claims totalling ₹229,005.39 |

**By category**

**By courier**

| Courier | Findings | Claim (₹) |
|---|---:|---:|
| C2 | 696 | 80,526.32 |
| C1 | 537 | 80,066.77 |
| C3 | 540 | 68,412.30 |

**Key findings**

- **Weight inflation is the biggest problem:** 60% of all findings and a third of the recoverable amount.
- **Unpaid COD is the biggest single-item risk:** 45 delivered parcels worth ₹58,203 have no remittance
  21+ days after the latest statement, an average of about ₹1,293 per parcel.
- **Every duplicate is a straight re-bill:** all 116 repeated lines carry exactly the same charges as the
  original and appear on a different invoice, so they are not separate RTO or COD bills being misread.
- **All 81 COD-fee findings are on prepaid parcels** (79 delivered, 2 returned), where no COD fee is due.
- **The money at stake is spread evenly across couriers:** C1 and C2 are almost level at about ₹80,000
  each, and C3 is at ₹68,000.

Merchant 1 ("Pune Threads", loaded by the M0 sample loader) has the same invoice lines but no ledger rows,
because its ledger has never been rebuilt. The audit therefore treats all 15,447 of its lines as not
verifiable and raises no findings, by design. Running `POST /api/ledger/rebuild/` and then
`POST /api/audit/run/` as that merchant would audit it.
