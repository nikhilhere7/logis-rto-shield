# M4 — RTO Prediction Model

M4 scores every order for its risk of coming back as an RTO (return to origin), using only what is known
when the order is placed. It trains on each merchant's own history from the M2 ledger, picks between a
logistic regression and LightGBM, calibrates the winner so its scores read as real probabilities, and
turns those probabilities into a rupee decision: which COD orders are worth stopping or confirming.

## 1. The business problem

An RTO costs the merchant twice: the courier bills forward freight and return freight, and the sale is
lost. Most of that loss sits in cash-on-delivery orders, where the customer has paid nothing up front. If
risky COD orders can be spotted at checkout, the merchant can ask for prepayment or a confirmation call
before shipping. The catch is that some of those flagged customers were going to accept the parcel, and a
share of them will walk away when asked. M4 measures both sides and recommends a cut-off.

## 3. `RiskModel` and artifact storage

`RiskModel` extends `core.TenantModel`, so it inherits the `merchant` foreign key and `for_merchant()`.

| Field | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `status` | `trained` or `insufficient_data` |
| `is_active` | The model currently in use (at most one per merchant) |
| `artifact` | File under `MEDIA_ROOT/models/`, e.g. `models/rto_m2_20261112080812.joblib` |
| `metrics` | JSON: everything the API reports (split, scores, bands, economics, top features) |
| `trained_at` | When the run finished |

Records are listed newest first. The artifact is a `joblib` file holding exactly
`{"scorer", "test", "rto_cost"}`: the fitted scorer (model + calibrator + training columns), the
test-period rows, and the average RTO cost. `load_artifact(run)` reads it back; the economics endpoint
uses it to recompute economics under different assumptions without retraining.

**Saving is all-or-nothing.** Training happens before anything is written. The artifact file is then
saved, and one database transaction creates the record, switches off the merchant's previous active
model and switches on the new one. If that transaction fails, the new file is deleted and the previous
model stays active. A run with too little data is stored as an inactive `insufficient_data` record and
never replaces the active model.

## 4. Features and leakage prevention

Every feature is something the merchant knows **at the moment the order is placed**.

| Group | Features |
|---|---|
| Order | `is_cod`, `order_value`, `order_hour`, `is_late_night` (22:00–05:59 IST), `is_weekend` (Sat/Sun IST) |
| Customer | `prior_orders`, `is_first_order`, `cust_prior_rto`, `cust_prior_delivered` |
| Destination | `pin_prior_parcels`, `pin_prior_rto_rate`, `city_tier` (missing → 2), `zone` |
| Address | `addr_len`, `addr_words`, `addr_commas`, `addr_has_number`, `addr_has_landmark`, `addr_is_lowercase` |
| Channel | `sales_channel` |

`zone` and `sales_channel` are one-hot encoded; everything else is numeric (`NUMERIC` and `CATEGORICAL`
in `features.py`). Each feature has a plain-English label in `LABELS` (e.g. `addr_is_lowercase` →
"Address typed carelessly"), which the API uses when it explains a model.

**Deliberately left out:** the courier (chosen after the order), weight and pickup, delivery attempts and
outcome flags, invoice and billing amounts, and COD remittance. `billed_total_inr` is carried in the
training frame only to price the economics; it never enters the model's inputs. Lost parcels are left out
of training because their outcome is neither delivered nor returned.

**Address features** come from `Customer.address_line`. The landmark check matches
`near|opp|opposite|behind|beside|next to|above|below` as whole words in any case. "Typed carelessly"
means the address has letters and none are capitals.

**Design matrix.** `design_matrix(d, columns)` turns numbers into floats and categories into one-hot
columns, drops blank categories, and when given the training columns, lines new rows up to exactly those
columns: an unseen zone or channel creates no new column and sets no existing one.

## 5. History without peeking into the future

The customer and pincode features count past outcomes. A past outcome may only count if it was **known
before** the order being scored, not merely placed before it.

- **When an outcome became known** (`resolved_ts`): the delivery time; otherwise the time the courier
  started the return (`Shipment.rto_initiated_ts`, which the ledger does not carry); otherwise 10 days
  after pickup.
- **Matching:** `pd.merge_asof(..., direction="backward", allow_exact_matches=False)`, per customer,
  per pincode and merchant-wide. An outcome known at exactly the moment an order is placed does not count.
- **Smoothing:** a pincode with few parcels is pulled towards the merchant's average:

  ```
  pin_prior_rto_rate = (pin RTOs + 20 × base_rate) / (pin parcels + 20)
  ```

  `base_rate` is the merchant-wide return rate of outcomes known before that order, so it too never uses
  the future. Before any outcome is known it is 0.2.

## 6. Training: two candidates, one winner

| Candidate | Set-up |
|---|---|
| Logistic regression | `StandardScaler` → `LogisticRegression(max_iter=1000)` |
| LightGBM | Native `lgb.train`: `learning_rate` 0.03, `num_leaves` 7, `min_data_in_leaf` 60, `lambda_l2` 5, `feature_fraction` 0.8, `bagging_fraction` 0.8 every round, `seed` 42, deterministic, one thread. Up to 2,000 rounds with early stopping after 100 rounds without improvement on the validation period |

Both are trained on the training period only. **The winner is chosen on validation AUC alone:** LightGBM
is used only if its validation AUC beats logistic regression's by at least **0.005**. The simpler model
wins ties, because it is easier to explain and less likely to overfit. The test period plays no part in
early stopping or selection.

A merchant needs at least **2,000** parcels with a known outcome and **200** RTOs to train. Below that,
or if any period contains only one kind of outcome, the run is saved as `insufficient_data` with the
counts and a reason.

## 7. The 70 / 15 / 15 time split

Rows are ordered by order time and never shuffled:

| Period | Share | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| Train | Oldest 70% | Fitting both candidates |
| Validation | Next 15% | Early stopping, choosing the winner, calibration |
| Test | Newest 15% | Reporting only |

This mirrors real use: the model is always judged on orders that came after everything it learned from.
The reported model is the one trained on the oldest 70%; it is not refitted on all data afterwards.

## 8. Calibration and evaluation

**Calibration.** A logistic regression is fitted on the logit of the winner's validation-period
predictions. Its output is the calibrated probability used everywhere else, so a score of 0.30 should
mean roughly 30 in 100 such orders come back.

**Metrics** (stored in `RiskModel.metrics`):

| Key | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `split` | Rows, date range and RTO rate (%) of each period |
| `candidates` | AUC, average precision and Brier for both models on validation and test (before calibration) |
| `chosen`, `lgb_best_iteration` | The winner, and where LightGBM's early stopping settled |
| `test_after_calibration` | AUC, average precision and Brier of the calibrated winner on the test period |
| `brier_if_we_predicted_the_average` | The Brier score of predicting the test period's average for everyone: the bar to beat |
| `top_10pct_of_orders_hold_pct_of_rto` | Share of all test-period RTOs found in the riskiest 10% of orders |
| `bands` | Calibration check: orders, average predicted and actual RTO rate in six score bands (0–5%, 5–10%, 10–20%, 20–35%, 35–50%, 50%+); empty bands are left out |
| `top_features` | The ten most important features, with their share of the model's total importance |
| `economics` | See section 10 |

AUC and average precision are rounded to 3 decimals, Brier to 4.

## 9. Explaining a score

The scorer can explain both the model and a single order:

- **Feature importance** (`top_features`): LightGBM's total gain per feature, or the size of each
  logistic coefficient, as a share of the total. One-hot columns are folded back into their feature, so
  all zones count as one "Delivery zone".
- **Contributions** (`Scorer.contributions`): how far each feature pushes one order's risk up or down, on
  the model's log-odds scale before calibration. LightGBM uses its built-in per-row breakdown; logistic
  regression uses each standardised value times its coefficient.
- **Labels**: one-hot columns read naturally, e.g. "Delivery zone: B".

## 10. COD threshold economics

For each cut-off from **0.10 to 0.80** in steps of 0.05, every **COD** order in the test period with a
calibrated score at or above the cut-off counts as flagged. Prepaid orders are ignored.

```
saved        = RTOs flagged × average RTO cost
margin lost  = Σ order value of good orders flagged × margin_pct / 100 × (1 − keep_rate)
net          = saved − margin lost
```

| Assumption | Default | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `rto_cost_inr` | From data | Average courier bill (`billed_total_inr`) on returned parcels in the **training** period |
| `margin_pct` | 30 | Profit margin on an order, % |
| `keep_rate` | 0.6 | Share of good customers who still go ahead after being flagged |

The output gives the assumptions; the COD orders, COD RTOs and their total RTO cost in the test period;
a table of `threshold, orders_flagged, rto_caught, good_orders_flagged, saved_inr, margin_lost_inr,
net_inr` (whole rupees); and `best`, the row with the highest positive net saving, or `null` if no cut-off
pays.

## 11. API

All endpoints use the project's `MerchantAPIView`: the user must be logged in, and the merchant always
comes from the logged-in user, never from the request.

| Method | Path | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| POST | `/api/ml/train/` | Trains on the merchant's current ledger and returns the run: `id`, `status`, `is_active`, `trained_at`, then every metric at the top level. Returns 200 for both `trained` and `insufficient_data`; check `status` |
| GET | `/api/ml/model/` | The active model; if there is none, the latest run (for example one with too little data); 404 if the merchant has never trained |
| GET | `/api/ml/economics/` | Economics for the active model, recomputed from its saved test rows. 404 if there is no active model |

Training runs inside the request and takes about a second on the current data. Uploading files never
starts training; it is always an explicit `POST /api/ml/train/`.

## 12. What-if economics

`GET /api/ml/economics/` accepts:

| Parameter | Default | Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| `margin_pct` | 30 | A number from 0 to 100 |
| `keep_rate` | 0.6 | A number from 0 to 1 |

A non-number or an out-of-range value returns 400. The model and its scores do not change; only the
money side is recalculated. Example: `/api/ml/economics/?margin_pct=40&keep_rate=0.5`.

`ml/test_ml.py` runs on pytest's isolated test database. Model training uses synthetic orders; the API
test uses the existing ingestion fixtures.

| Train | 2024-01-01 → 2025-08-10, 10,723 orders, 20.6% RTO |
| Validation | 2025-08-11 → 2025-10-24, 2,298 orders, 12.4% RTO |
| Test | 2025-10-24 → 2025-12-31, 2,298 orders, 11.2% RTO |

**Model selection (validation period)**

**Test period, after calibration**

| Measure | Value |
|---|---|
| AUC | **0.841** |
| Average precision | **0.431** (the test RTO rate is 0.112) |
| Brier | **0.0789**, against **0.0997** for predicting the average |
| Riskiest 10% of orders | hold **41.9%** of all RTOs |

**Calibration bands (test period)**

| Score band | Orders | Avg predicted | Actual RTO rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0–5% | 1,370 | 1.4% | 2.7% |
| 5–10% | 187 | 7.3% | 8.6% |
| 10–20% | 283 | 15.0% | 14.8% |

| 35–50% | 108 | 42.2% | 47.2% |
| 50%+ | 94 | 59.8% | 55.3% |

**Most important features:** cash on delivery (26.4%), customer's earlier orders (15.0%), address parts
(13.6%), first-time customer (9.0%), delivery zone (7.6%), words in address (6.9%).

**Economics (test period: 908 COD orders, 215 RTOs, ₹95,056 of RTO cost at ₹442.12 each)**

| Assumptions | Best cut-off | Flagged | RTOs caught | Good orders flagged | Saved | Margin lost | **Net** |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Default: 30% margin, 0.6 keep rate | 0.35 | 202 | 103 | 99 | ₹45,538 | ₹20,752 | **₹24,787** |
| 40% margin, 0.5 keep rate | 0.40 | 166 | 89 | 77 | ₹39,349 | ₹24,909 | **₹14,440** |

With the default assumptions, flagging at 0.10 or 0.15 loses money: too many good orders are caught up.
A dearer margin and a lower keep rate push the best cut-off up and roughly halve the net saving, which is
why the endpoint lets the merchant enter their own numbers.

## 15. Limitations

- **Synthetic or derived data.** The dataset was generated or derived for this project rather than
  exported from a live store. Its patterns, such as how strongly COD and address quality drive returns,
  may be cleaner or stronger than in real orders. Results on a real merchant may be weaker.
- **RTO drift.** The RTO rate falls from 20.6% in the training period to 11.2% in the test period. The
  model's ranking held up (test AUC 0.841), and calibration on the more recent validation period keeps
  scores close to actual rates, but a model trained on one period can drift as the business changes.
  It should be retrained regularly.
- **Address history.** The customer table stores only each customer's latest address, so older orders
  from repeat customers are scored with today's address. There is no per-order address in the schema.
- **Cancelled orders.** `prior_orders` and `is_first_order` come from the M2 ledger, which counts
  cancelled orders as earlier orders.
- **Back-test savings are not a promise.** The rupee figures replay one past period under fixed
  assumptions. Actual savings depend on how customers respond when flagged, real margins, courier costs,
  and future order patterns. The `keep_rate` in particular is an assumption, not something measured.
- **Small calibration bands.** The highest score bands hold relatively few orders, so their actual RTO
  rates are noisier than the lower bands.
- **Training is synchronous.** It runs inside the API request. That is fine at the current size but would
  need a background job for much larger merchants.
