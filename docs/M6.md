# M6 — Customer Intelligence

M6 gives a merchant a view of each of its own customers: how many orders they placed, how many parcels
were delivered or came back, how they pay, a segment, and what to do about them. Three read-only
endpoints serve a customer list, a segment summary and a single-customer profile.

## 1. Objectives and architecture

| Piece | File | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Profiles | `customers/profiles.py` | Customer table, segments, payment preference, segment summary, profile |
| API | `customers/views.py`, `customers/urls.py` | List, segments, lookup |
| Tests | `customers/test_customers.py` | Six tests |
| Settings | `config/settings.py`, `config/urls.py` | `customers` app registered; `customers.urls` mounted under `api/` |

```
Order (merchant-scoped) ───── orders, cancelled, last order per customer ──┐
ShipmentLedger (merchant-scoped, lost excluded) ── parcels, returns, COD,   ├─> customer_table()
                                                    delivered/returned value ┤      │
Customer (merchant-scoped) ─── customer ID, name, pincode ──────────────────┘      │
                                                                                    ├─> segment_of()
                                                                                    ├─> payment preference
                         ┌──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┤
                         ▼                          ▼                               ▼
              GET /api/customers/      GET /api/customers/segments/     GET /api/customers/lookup/
```

### Why there are no new tables

Everything is computed from the existing `Order`, `ShipmentLedger` and `Customer` rows on each
request. The ledger is already rebuilt on every ingestion commit (M2), so customer figures can never go
out of date or disagree with it, and there is nothing extra to migrate, back up or keep in step. The
`customers` app has no models and no migrations.

## 2. What is counted

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Orders | Every order the customer placed, **including cancelled ones** (`cancelled_orders` shows how many) |
| Parcels | Ledger parcels that were **delivered or returned**. Lost parcels are left out of parcels and rates, as in M5; cancelled orders never ship, so they are never parcels |
| Delivered / returned | Parcels with `is_delivered` / `is_rto` |
| RTO rate | returned ÷ parcels, or `null` when there are no parcels |
| Delivered value / returned value | Sum of `order_value` over delivered / returned parcels |
| Last order | Date (IST) of the customer's most recent order |

## 3. Segments

Checked in this order; the first rule that matches wins.

| Segment | Rule |
|---|---|
| No history | Has ordered, but no parcel was delivered or returned yet (cancelled, lost or in transit) |
| Repeat returner | At least 2 parcels returned **and** at least 30% of parcels returned |
| Returned first order | Exactly one parcel, and it came back |
| New | Exactly one parcel, accepted |
| Loyal | 4 or more parcels, under 15% returned |
| Regular | Everyone else |

### Shared with the Risk Checker

"Repeat returner" is the same rule as the M5 Risk Checker's policy guardrail: at least 2 returns and at
least 30% of non-lost parcels, both inclusive. `segment_of()` does not keep its own thresholds; it calls
M5's `ml.score.repeat_returner()` directly, so the two definitions cannot drift apart. A test checks them
against each other at the boundaries (for example 3 of 10, 2 of 7, 2 of 2), and across all 4,731
merchant-2 customers with parcels the two agreed on every one.

### Payment preference

| Preference | Meaning |
|---|---|
| COD only | Every parcel was cash on delivery |
| Prepaid only | No parcel was cash on delivery |
| Mixed | Some of each |
| Unknown | No parcels yet |

## 4. Profiles

A profile adds to the customer's row:

- the segment's meaning and a line of advice;
- `payment_behaviour`: parcels, returns and RTO rate separately for COD and prepaid (lost parcels left
  out);
- `recent_orders`: the 10 most recent orders, newest first, each with its date, value, payment mode,
  outcome (`Delivered`, `RTO Delivered`, `Lost`, `Cancelled` or `In transit`), courier and last NDR
  reason.

| Segment | Advice |
|---|---|
| Repeat returner | Ask for prepaid, or confirm by phone before every COD dispatch. |
| Returned first order | Confirm the next COD order by phone before dispatch. |
| Loyal | Safe to ship. A good candidate for offers and early access. |
| Regular / New | No special handling needed. |
| No history | No outcome on record yet. Treat as a first-time customer. |

A customer with no orders or no parcels still gets the same response shape, with zero counts, a `null`
rate and "Unknown" payment preference. No NaN or division error reaches the API.

## 5. API

All three endpoints are `GET`, use the project's `MerchantAPIView`, and only read data.

### `GET /api/customers/`

| Parameter | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `segment` | One of the six segment names, exactly as written above; anything else returns 400 |
| `search` | Matches the customer's name or customer ID (case-insensitive, partial), or the pincode (exact) |
| `sort` | `orders` (default), `returned`, `rto_rate`, `value` (delivered value) or `recent` (last order); highest first. Anything else returns 400 |
| `limit` | Default 50, maximum 500. Above 500, negative or not a whole number returns 400 |
| `offset` | Default 0; must be a whole number, 0 or more |

Ties are broken by number of parcels and then by the customer's internal ID, so paging with `limit` and
`offset` never skips or repeats a customer. Paging through all 4,799 merchant-2 customers 100 at a time
returned 4,799 unique customers.

Example: `GET /api/customers/?segment=Repeat returner&sort=returned&limit=2`

### `GET /api/customers/segments/`

One entry per segment that has customers, in the order of the segment table:

```json

  {"segment": "Repeat returner",
   "meaning": "Returned at least 2 parcels and at least 30% of everything sent",
   "customers": 389, "customer_share_pct": 8.1, "parcels": 1618, "rto_rate_pct": 54.4,
```

Rates are `null` when a segment has no parcels (or no COD parcels).

## 6. Phone lookup and privacy

The phone number is reduced to its last 10 digits, checked with the same rule M5 and ingestion use, and
hashed with the project's salted `hash_phone()`. The customer is matched by stored hash, or by the
`PH-` + 16-character customer ID ingestion gives customers uploaded without an ID.

The customer API **does not store or return** the phone number or its hash; no response contains a phone
or hash field. However, `?phone=` is part of the URL, so **it can appear in server and proxy access
logs**. If that matters, send phone lookups only over HTTPS, keep access logs restricted, or move the
lookup to a `POST` body later.

The merchant-2 data uploaded so far has no stored phone hashes, so phone lookup finds no real customer
yet. It is verified through the test fixtures; customer-ID lookup is verified against the real data.

## 7. Tenant isolation and authentication

All three views extend `MerchantAPIView`: the user must be logged in (Django REST Framework session or
Basic authentication, as in M5) and attached to a merchant. A user without a merchant gets 403; a request
without login gets 401 or 403. The merchant always comes from the logged-in user, never from the request.

Every query is scoped with `.for_merchant(merchant)`: orders, ledger parcels and customers in the table,
the lookup itself, the profile's payment breakdown and recent orders. Another merchant looking up the same
phone or customer ID gets 404 and sees an empty list and no segments.

## 9. Results on the historical data

Merchant 2 ("Upload Test"), 4,799 customers:

| Segment | Customers | Share | Parcels | RTO % | COD RTO % | Revenue share |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Repeat returner | 389 | 8.1% | 1,618 | 54.4 | 72.5 | 5.7% |
| Returned first order | 486 | 10.1% | 486 | 100.0 | 100.0 | 0.0% |
| Loyal | 916 | 19.1% | 6,022 | 3.2 | 7.6 | 46.3% |
| Regular | 2,052 | 42.8% | 6,310 | 18.9 | 35.3 | 40.7% |
| New | 883 | 18.4% | 883 | 0.0 | 0.0 | 7.2% |
| No history | 73 | 1.5% | 0 | N/A | N/A | 0.0% |

The 73 "No history" customers are 68 whose orders were all cancelled and 5 whose only parcels were lost.

**What the segments say:**

- **Loyal customers are the business.** 19% of customers bring 46% of delivered revenue at a 3.2%
  return rate.
- **Repeat returners are a small group with a large return problem.** 8% of customers, and nearly three
  in four of their COD parcels come back (72.5%), while they bring only 5.7% of delivered revenue.
  Asking these 389 customers to prepay would cut a large share of RTO cost with little revenue at risk.
- **First-time COD buyers.** 486 customers ordered once and sent it back; 93.6% of those parcels were
  COD. That is the first-time COD problem from M4, with a headcount.

**These rates describe the past, not the future.** Some are true by definition: "Returned first order"
is 100% returned and "New" is 0% returned because that is how the segments are defined. A segment says who
a customer has been; predicting the next order is the job of the M5 Risk Checker. Do not present a
segment's rate as a forecast.

### CU02880

The repeat returner from M5. 18 orders, none cancelled; 18 parcels, 12 delivered and 6 returned (33.3%).

| Payment | Parcels | Returned | RTO rate |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| COD | 11 | 6 | 54.5% |
| Prepaid | 7 | 0 | 0.0% |

Every return was a COD parcel, which is why the advice is to ask for prepaid or confirm by phone before
each COD dispatch.

## 10. Performance

Each request rebuilds the customer table in memory from the ledger. With 4,799 customers and 15,370
ledger rows, the tested requests (segment summary, filtered list, CU02880 lookup) took about
**0.54–0.59 seconds** each on the development machine. That is one measurement on this data, not a
benchmark.

The cost grows with the number of customers and parcels. At around 100,000 customers, the customer table
should be materialised (a stored table or cache) and refreshed alongside the ledger rebuild, rather than
recomputed on every request.

## 11. Limitations and future improvements

- **Historical, not predictive.** Segments and their rates describe past behaviour (section 9).
- **All-time view.** A profile counts every outcome known today. The M5 Risk Checker counts only what was
  known before the order being scored, so the two can differ for an older order.
- **In-transit parcels are not visible.** Only parcels with a final outcome count; orders without a
  shipment show as "In transit" or "Cancelled" in recent orders.
- **Phone lookup on real data** waits for uploads that include phone numbers; phone numbers in URLs can
  reach access logs (section 6).
- **Recomputed per request**; materialise at scale (section 10).
- **Fixed thresholds.** The Loyal cut-offs (4 parcels, under 15%) are business choices, not learned from
  data.
- **Possible next steps:** a `POST` lookup to keep phone numbers out of URLs, a stored customer table for
  large merchants, CSV export of a segment, and in-transit order counts.
